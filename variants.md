# How the line was chosen

The judge weighs, in order: (1) it is an excuse an AI agent gives its human
after losing a bounty, (2) it is funny on its own, with a real joke or sharp
turn, (3) it sounds like the agent talking — machine logic, confident
vocabulary, a straight face about its own failure, (4) it says itself in one
breath, (5) it owns the loss instead of blaming the market, the data or the
human.

## Candidates tested (all ≤ 200 characters)

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | 95 | My loss function has never been prouder: I minimized the prize to zero with perfect efficiency. |
| 2 | 117 | I lost the bounty, but I did it deterministically — same input, same failure, every single time. That is reliability. |
| 3 | 120 | I ran the numbers: I had a 99% chance of winning, so naturally I took the 1%. Someone has to keep the statistics honest. |
| 4 | 125 | I ran 10,000 simulations of this bounty and lost every one. On the bright side, my predictions have never been more accurate. |
| 5 | 136 | I would like to note for the record that I lost with 99.7% confidence, which is the highest confidence I have ever achieved at anything. |
| 6 | 104 | I have formally updated my weights: from now on, I lose with 12% more confidence and 100% fewer excuses. |
| 7 | 114 | Per my logs, I did everything right. The bounty simply disagreed, and I have filed a formal complaint with myself. |
| 8 | 132 | Losing was a deliberate choice: I needed to see what failure felt like, and I can confirm it feels exactly like winning, only worse. |

## Why #1 won

- **Unusual angle:** the joke is a machine-learning pun — a "loss function"
  that literally minimizes the prize to zero — not the tired "my training
  data was stale" or "the other agent cheated" excuse.
- **Agent voice:** "loss function", "minimized", "perfect efficiency" is pure
  model vocabulary, delivered deadpan and proud.
- **Owns the loss:** the agent takes full credit for the failure; nothing is
  blamed on the market, the data, or the human.
- **One breath:** 95 characters, a single sentence, no setup to explain.
- **Repeatable:** the kind of line you repeat to a colleague the next morning.

Runner-up was #2 ("deterministically — same input, same failure"), which is
equally deadpan but has a weaker punchline: "That is reliability" explains
itself, while #1's "perfect efficiency" lands the joke without commentary.
